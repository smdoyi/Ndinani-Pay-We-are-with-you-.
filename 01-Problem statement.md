Elderly and visually impaired individuals are largely excluded from digital banking because existing apps fail to accommodate their needs. Many elderly users struggle with English-only interfaces and must rely on third parties to translate or navigate the app on their behalf, compromising both their independence and the privacy of their financial information. Visually impaired users face a similar barrier: without screen-reading or voice-guided functionality, they cannot see or navigate the app at all, forcing them to depend on someone else to complete even basic transactions. Current banking solutions were not designed with these users in mind, leaving a significant portion of the population unable to bank safely and independently.

Ndinani Pay solves this by providing a voice-guided fintech app that allows visually impaired users to navigate every function independently, without third-party assistance. Users register and set up their profile in person at a traditional bank branch, ensuring proper verification and support during onboarding, and from that point on can use the app entirely on their own. To address the language barrier for elderly users, the app supports isiXhosa, English, and Afrikaans , the three dominant languages in the Eastern Cape, where the platform was first launched ,  with plans to expand language support as the app grows into new regions.
